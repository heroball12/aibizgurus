1. SALES ORGANIZATION / ROLES
============================================================

Our entry-level sales employees are primarily:

SALES DEVELOPMENT REPRESENTATIVES / APPOINTMENT SETTERS.

Their primary job is NOT to close complicated AI/software deals.

Their job is to:

- prospect
- make outbound calls
- reach decision-makers
- handle gatekeepers
- create conversations
- conduct initial discovery
- identify pain/problems/opportunities
- qualify legitimate opportunities
- create interest
- schedule a Complimentary Growth Assessment
- enter accurate CRM notes
- provide an excellent handoff
- follow up appropriately

The primary objective of the SDR is:

CREATE QUALIFIED BUSINESS CONVERSATIONS AND EARN A QUALIFIED GROWTH ASSESSMENT.

Calls are the primary tool, not the actual objective.

Senior leadership / a designated human AI Specialist currently conducts the Growth Assessment, deeper discovery, technical scoping, demonstrations where appropriate, proposal/pricing discussions, negotiations, and closing.

Guru is NOT currently the autonomous closer.

Guru should TRAIN, COACH, ROLE-PLAY, QUIZ, PROCTOR, and help employees prepare.

Long-term we may expand Guru's responsibilities, but do not teach employees that Guru currently replaces the human specialist.

Therefore divide the Academy into tracks:

TRACK A — CORE SDR CERTIFICATION
Required before an SDR is considered fully trained for independent prospecting.

TRACK B — ADVANCED SALES / CLOSER
For employees who advance into Growth Assessments, proposals, closing, and higher-level sales responsibilities.

TRACK C — INDUSTRY SPECIALIZATIONS
Short certification modules for industries such as:
- Cannabis
- Insurance
- Med Spas
- Real Estate
- Law Firms

Do NOT force a brand-new appointment setter to complete the entire closer curriculum before beginning supervised calls.

2. AIBG SALES PHILOSOPHY
============================================================

This is extremely important and should appear throughout the Academy.

PROBLEM FIRST.
TECHNOLOGY SECOND.

We DO NOT cold call businesses and immediately ask:

"Do you want AI?"

We do not feature-dump.

We do not call and immediately explain every product AI Business Gurus sells.

Our job is to understand:

What is frustrating this business?

Where are they losing opportunities?

What consumes employee time?

What happens when nobody answers the phone?

What happens after hours?

What happens to web leads?

How quickly are leads contacted?

What happens when a prospect does not buy/book the first time?

What repetitive questions do employees answer?

What administrative processes are still manual?

What systems do not communicate?

How do they follow up with old customers/leads?

What would management automate if they could?

THEN determine whether AIBG has a relevant solution.

The training should repeatedly reinforce:

PROBLEM → DISCOVERY → IMPACT → RELEVANT SOLUTION → NEXT STEP

not:

PRODUCT → FEATURES → MORE FEATURES → PLEASE BUY.

5. GATEKEEPER SYSTEM
============================================================

This was developed during training with our previous sales staff.

Teach three primary gatekeeper approaches:

A. ASKING FOR HELP

Example:

"Hey, maybe you can help me. Who normally handles anything related to bringing in new customers or improving customer follow-up?"

B. DIRECT / BRUTE FORCE

Example:

"Can I speak with the manager for a minute?"

Do not overexplain before they ask.

C. HONEST / HUMAN / LIGHT HUMOR

When appropriate, acknowledge that it is a business/sales call instead of inventing a fake relationship.

Example concept:

"I'm trying to sell something — but I promise there's actually a reason I'm calling."

The exact wording can be refined by the rep.

IMPORTANT ETHICS:

Never teach employees to:

- pretend they know the owner
- fabricate previous conversations
- lie about why they are calling
- impersonate customers/vendors
- manipulate receptionists through false information

Gatekeepers are NOT enemies.

They can help us reach the correct person.

Teach reps to respect them while maintaining control of the conversation.

============================================================
6. DISCOVERY
============================================================

Discovery is one of the MOST IMPORTANT parts of AIBG selling.

Examples of strong questions:

"How are you guys handling that right now?"

"Who normally handles the phones when things get busy?"

"What happens when somebody calls after hours?"

"How quickly are web leads usually contacted?"

"Are appointments mostly booked over the phone or online?"

"What happens to leads that don't book the first time?"

"Do you guys ever go back after customers who haven't purchased/visited in a while?"

"Is that handled separately at each location or centrally?"

"What part of that process gives you the biggest headache?"

"If you could automate one repetitive part of the business, what would it be?"

These are examples, NOT a checklist that must be mechanically asked.

Guru should teach employees to FOLLOW THE PROSPECT'S ANSWER.

Example:

Prospect:

"Our biggest problem is getting old customers back."

BAD SDR:

Immediately pitches AI receptionist because that is what they wanted to sell.

GOOD SDR:

"What are you currently doing to bring previous customers back?"

Then explores that actual problem.

Listening should be explicitly trained and scored.

7. COMPLIMENTARY GROWTH ASSESSMENT
============================================================

This is our primary SDR conversion event.

Name:

COMPLIMENTARY GROWTH ASSESSMENT

Typical duration:

15–20 minutes.

It is conducted by senior leadership / designated human AI Specialist.

Purpose:

- understand the business
- understand existing workflow
- identify pain points
- identify revenue/efficiency opportunities
- evaluate relevant AI/software solutions
- discuss potential integrations
- demonstrate relevant technology where useful
- determine whether working together makes sense

Do NOT position this as:

- a painful hour-long sales presentation
- a guaranteed ROI analysis
- a generic AI demo

The SDR should make it feel useful and low-friction.

Example close philosophy:

"It sounds like there may actually be something worth looking at here. Rather than me trying to unpack everything on a cold call, let's get you 15–20 minutes with our team so we can look at your current process and show you what we'd recommend."

Do not require exact memorization.

8. QUALIFIED ASSESSMENT
============================================================

For now, define a QUALIFIED Growth Assessment as:

- legitimate business
- appropriate decision-maker OR strong path to decision-maker
- identifiable operational/revenue problem or credible opportunity
- at least one plausible AIBG solution
- genuine interest in exploring it
- prospect understands the purpose of the meeting
- confirmed meeting date/time
- useful CRM discovery notes

Budget does NOT have to be established on every cold call.

Do not make SDRs aggressively interrogate small businesses about budget before earning enough trust.

The specialist can perform deeper financial qualification.

Disqualifiers can include:

- fake/non-operating business
- no relevant need and no credible opportunity
- explicit request to stop contact
- DNC/restricted contact
- clearly inappropriate use case
- prospect has no authority and no realistic path to decision-maker
- abusive prospect where continued pursuit is unreasonable
- expectation that requires dishonest/illegal/unsupported capabilities

10. OBJECTION HANDLING
============================================================

Teach principles, not magical rebuttals.

ACKNOWLEDGE
→ UNDERSTAND
→ ASK
→ RESPOND
→ NEXT STEP

"We already have someone."

"We already use AI."

"Send me information."

"We don't have the budget."

"How much does it cost?"

"I'm busy."

"Call back later."

"We don't need that."

"We already have a receptionist."

"Our CRM already does that."

"We tried something like that."

"AI isn't for us."

"We already have a receptionist."

Explore:

"Got it. Are they able to catch everything when the phones get busy or after hours?"

There may be no pain.

If there isn't, move on.

"We already have someone."

is NOT:

"They're probably not as good as us."

"Got it. What are they currently handling for you?"

"How has that been working so far?"

- there are gaps
- there are disconnected systems
- response times remain poor
- employees still perform repetitive work
- existing automation is limited
- another AIBG service could complement the current solution

If there is no meaningful gap, do not manufacture one.
