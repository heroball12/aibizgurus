ERYKHA'S FIELD GUIDE / AUTOMOTIVE

Problem first.
Let the experience do the talking.

Ask for the BDC Director, Internet Director, GSM, GM or Dealer Principal.

“I actually spent about 14 years in automotive and BDC, so there's a specific reason I'm here. I work with AI Business Gurus, helping teams look at where AI could support their current process.”

Find the bottleneck.

  • What CRM are you using?
  • Centralized BDC or floor-managed leads?
  • How fast are fresh internet leads contacted?
  • What happens after hours?
  • How long do you continue following up?
  • Are you actively mining old leads?
  • Who handles confirmations? How are show rates?
  • Does service have its own BDC?

Choose one relevant demo.

  • Slow response → Fresh internet lead
  • After-hours gaps → After-hours, 11:47 PM
  • Old database → Database reactivation
  • Missed calls → Missed-call scenario; future phone concept
  • Service workload → Service intake & repair orders
  • Inventory questions → Vehicle shopping
  • Appointment friction → Test drive

The 3–5 minute walkthrough.

  1. Open Automotive → Demo controls → choose the pain point.
  2. “Honestly, it's easier if I show you. Talk to it like you'd talk to one of your BDC reps.”
  3. Let them ask about a three-row AWD SUV under $50,000. Follow their interests.
  4. Try a trade or a demo appointment. Use fictional details.
  5. Tap Show dealership view → Enter the dealership CRM. Show the captured customer, booking, summary and activity. “This is what your team gets.”
  6. “This same intelligence can live on your website, phones and follow-up workflows, depending on your systems and implementation.”

Show the service department.

  1. Select Service beside Vehicle sales. Ask Axel for an oil change and brake inspection using a fictional vehicle, name and contact details.
  2. Give mileage and a drop-off preference. Review the intake form or continue talking, then choose an offered demo intake time.
  3. Open Service CRM. Show the same customer, vehicle, concern, appointment and numbered service order.
  4. As the service advisor, move through check-in, inspection and awaiting approval. Describe the fictional work, assign a technician and record simulated customer approval before starting work.
  5. Mark the order ready for pickup, return to Axel and ask for its status. He reads the same saved order. Close the order to finish the workflow.

The service CRM is session-scoped. No real repair, charge, customer notification or external dealer integration takes place.
